# LinkedIn post: Africa was never small—the map made it look small

On 4 September 2026, the UN General Assembly adopted the **Correct the Map** resolution by **164 votes to 1, with 6 abstentions**. The African-led initiative was introduced by Togo on behalf of the African Group and backed by the African Union.

Mercator was designed for navigation, not for comparing continental area. Because its scale increases toward the poles, Greenland, Europe, and northern North America are enlarged much more than equatorial Africa.

Our analysis used the same Natural Earth dataset across WGS 84, Web Mercator, and equal-area coordinate systems. It found:

- Africa: **29.95 million km²**
- Greenland: **2.21 million km²**
- United States: **9.51 million km²**
- Africa is **13.6× the area of Greenland**
- Greenland's Web Mercator inflation: **16.44×**, compared with **1.12×** for Africa
- On the Web Mercator comparison, Africa appears only about **0.9× as large as Greenland**

This is about more than mapmaking. The vote shows wide international support for the African Union's call to represent Africa fairly. It asks schools, publishers, UN bodies, media, and technology companies to use equal-area maps when comparing size and to teach that every flat map has limits.

The recommendation is practical: use **Equal Earth or another equal-area projection** for classroom world maps, data maps, and global size comparisons. Keep Mercator for navigation and detailed local web maps. Always name the projection, and use an equal-area map when calculating land area.

The resolution is a recommendation, not a law, and it changes no borders. What it can change is how students understand the world, how organizations choose maps, and how Africa is represented in global knowledge.

No flat map is perfect. The right principle is simple: **choose the projection that fits the purpose—and when comparing area, show Africa at its true relative scale.**
